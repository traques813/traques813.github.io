Unit 2 examined how structured data formats behave once loaded into Python, highlighting how their internal organisation shapes the required parsing strategy. CSV files map neatly to tables, JSON closely matches Python’s dictionary–list structures, and XML requires tree traversal through tags and attributes (Bird, Klein & Loper, 2009).  

XML, in particular, demonstrated how nested hierarchies demand careful inspection before extraction. Identifying how elements such as *Category* and *Code* combine to form meaningful key–value pairs illustrated why parsing depends as much on understanding layout as on syntax (Harold & Means, 2002).

Tools like `pprint` made it easier to inspect intermediate results. This helped verify that extraction logic was correct before constructing the final dictionary or table—an important step given that malformed structures or unexpected nesting are common in real-world data.

Overall, this unit showed that despite structural differences, CSV, JSON and XML can all be transformed into consistent Python structures once their patterns are clearly identified. This principle later shaped automated cleaning tasks in subsequent units.
